About

Engineer and astrophysicist.

I like complex problems and things that work reliably. I have worked in both academic research and industrial system verification, two very different worlds that taught me different things. I work well on my own and in teams, and I have had the chance to lead others, which I enjoy.

Journey

How I got here.

  1. Motala

    The adrenaline years

    I grew up in Motala, a small lakeside town in Sweden, and spent most of my time skateboarding, doing gymnastics and moving around. Those years taught me practice, persistence and not being afraid to fail in front of an audience. I left home at 20 to study physics, and the mindset came along: push, get back up, improve.

  2. Lund

    Physics and the cosmos

    Physics and then astrophysics at Lund University, learning how stars, clusters and planets behave. Physics taught me to think carefully, question assumptions and accept the result when a calculation shows something unexpected.

  3. Leiden

    Research abroad

    Doctoral research at Leiden Observatory on star–planet interaction. The work demanded independence: nobody had solved exactly that problem before. I published a first-author paper and learned to present technical ideas clearly. I left the programme before the defence, when we had children and wanted to move home to Sweden.

  4. Sweden

    Engineering and verification

    Back home I moved into system verification, first on assignment at Saab Dynamics and now as a consultant. The problems are more constrained than in research and the stakes are clearer: you verify that a system does what it should, which takes rigour and pragmatism. I have led small teams, managed requirements and worked on systems that matter to the people who use them.

  5. Home

    Family and balance

    I am married with two kids, and being present for them matters more to me than any title. I still skateboard when I can, produce music and build side projects in code. I like to understand how things work, whether that is a system, a piece of software or something I am just curious about.

What drives me

Four things I keep coming back to.

  • Clarity and rigour

    Vague requirements lead to confusion. I prefer explicit specifications and honest talk about what is and is not possible.

  • Learning by doing

    Reading about something is useful. You understand it when you implement it or debug it.

  • Good engineering

    Systems deserve care: failure modes thought through, decisions written down, things built to be maintained.

  • Balance

    A good career is part of a good life, not all of it. I take the work seriously, and I go home.
